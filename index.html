Hi. We are the ABCs of Data Science (Anika, Bea, & Carl).

And this is our project, Ship Happens: A Comprehensive Study on Port Activity in the Philippines. This study aims to identify the busiest ports in the Philippines, understand trends in port activity from 2019 to 2025, and develop a predictive model for shipping activity.

Here's an overview of our project.

Analyzing port activity to promote sustainable consumption and resource efficiency.

Motivation

Aligned with SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production), this project focuses on waste reduction, sustainable consumption, and resource efficiency in maritime logistics.

Problem

What are the busiest ports and when is traffic highest? We hypothesize that Manila is the busiest, peaking during the Christmas season and causing national delays.

Solution

By identifying busy ports, understanding trends from 2019-2025, and creating a predictive model, we aim to optimize shipping schedules and resource allocation.

We explored PortWatch's daily port activity data.

Source

PortWatch via Humanitarian Data Exchange (HDX).

Daily Port Activity

Philippine Daily Port Activity and Shipment Estimates.

Period

Daily data from January 1, 2019 to November 28, 2025.

Relevant features of the Portwatch data

Daily Vessel Traffic

Are counted as "port calls" when a vessel enters a predefined geofenced area (the port boundary).

Daily Trade Volume Estimates

The volume of import and export shipments in each of the ports are estimated in metric tons.

Hypothesis Testing Results

Hypotheses:
  1. The busiest shipping port in the Philippines is Manila.
  2. Port activity peaks during the Christmas season (November-December), causing shipping delays throughout the country because of higher demand.

We used the Kruskal-Wallis Test to check for significant differences in the variance of more than three independent groups—specifically, port activity across different months and ports. This non-parametric test is suitable for comparing multiple groups when the assumptions of ANOVA are not met.

Monthly Port Activity
Test Kruskal-Wallis H-test
H-statistic 0.6930
p-value 0.9999
Significance Level 0.05
Total Cargo Across Ports
Test Kruskal-Wallis H-test
H-statistic 300.0777
p-value 3.6543×10−49
Significance Level 0.05

Since the p-value is much greater than 0.05, there is no significant difference in port activity across the months.

The hypothesis that December is the busiest month for ports is not supported and is rejected.

Since the p-value is vastly less than 0.05, there is a highly significant difference in total cargo activity across different ports.

The hypothesis that Manila Port is the busiest port is supported and is not rejected.

To relate this to the visual results, where August is the busiest month for ports, this suggests that any increased activity in August is not universal, and not enough to make it stand out from the rest when grouped by month. Instead, this may be due to the significantly greater volume of cargo from Manila Port that caused the total monthly cargo across all ports to be greatest in August.

Machine Learning Modelling

Model Metrics

Predicting the exact port activity is difficult, but our model can predict whether a port will have more than the usual activity.

Model Architecture

The model uses Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks to capture temporal patterns in the data.

Model Training

The model was developed using tensorflow and trained on the Portwatch dataset. Extra features such as day of the week, holidays, and quarter were inferred to improve model performance.

Model Inference

The model uses the last 30 days of data to predict the next day's port activity. The model can predict whether a port will have more than the usual activity, which can help in optimizing shipping schedules.

Model Results

The model could theoretically predict the exact traffic of a certain port but this just means that it was able to understand the trends in the data.

More Results

When looking at the entire dataset, the model is not the most reliable to predict the exact traffic of a certain port. The model had a loss value of 0.0009, 0.0002, and 0.0008 (scaled MSE) for imports, exports, and port calls respectively.

Model Hit Rate

The average model hit rate across all ports is around 70-80%, which means that the model can predict whether a port will have more than the usual activity with reasonable accuracy.

Limitations and Recommendations

  Even though the model yielded a low loss value across all the predictive outcomes, this was merely caused by the large amounts of data. Another reason for the low loss values is that a lot of the ports in the data have little to no activity the entire time. The model was correctly able to determine which ports exhibited this behavior. The erratic behavior of data was sometimes captured by the model, when the behavior is sustained for a long time. For short periods and of spikes in the activity, the model had a difficult time of capturing it.

  A bit of feature engineering was done to improve the model's performance, such as inferring extra features like day of the week, holidays, and quarter. However, there are still many other features that can be inferred from the data, such as weather conditions, economic indicators, and global events, which can further improve the model's performance. The model can also be improved by using more advanced architectures such as attention mechanisms or transformer models, which can better capture the temporal dependencies in the data.
